Office Park Plumbing: Scale Changes Everything

A corporate restroom core on three floors, a cafeteria kitchen serving lunch to four hundred people, locker rooms and fitness centers — Eastgate office buildings concentrate more wastewater in one structure than an entire residential block. The upside of that scale is redundancy and access: commercial buildings have cleanouts, mechanical rooms, and as-built drawings. The downside is consequence: when a main backs up in a building with five hundred occupants, it's an event, not an inconvenience.

We maintain several Eastgate office properties on scheduled programs: semiannual jetting of kitchen and main lines, annual camera verification, and storm system cleanouts timed to the fall rains. Facility managers like it because it's predictable — fixed scope, fixed price, documented results — and because it converts plumbing from a source of 6 a.m. emergency calls into a line item that just gets handled.

Freeway-Adjacent Drainage Takes a Beating

Properties next to I-90 and I-405 deal with drainage stresses that inland sites never see. Highway runoff — carrying de-icing chemicals, tire residue, and fine sediment — sheets across frontage areas during storms. Vibration from heavy truck traffic compacts soil around buried lines over the years, stressing joints. And the sheer volume of impervious surface means storm systems here must move enormous quantities of water in a hurry.

We've seen Eastgate storm lines half-choked with the fine silt that washes off highway corridors — not dramatic debris, just years of accumulation narrowing the pipe a millimeter at a time until a big storm overwhelms it. The fix is jetting on a schedule, not when it floods. For properties within a few hundred yards of the freeway, we generally recommend storm system service every year without fail; the sediment load simply doesn't give you the multi-year grace period that quieter streets enjoy.

Parking Structures Have Hidden Drains

Eastgate's office parks and retail centers include multi-level parking structures, and their drainage is out of sight and out of mind — until it isn't. Deck drains clog with the same leaf litter, trash, and sediment as surface lots, but with an added twist: the drains run through the structure itself, so a backup doesn't just pond water, it sends it cascading to the level below. We've responded to garage floods where the actual clog was two floors up, in a drain nobody had looked at in years.

Structured parking drainage needs the same fall service as surface lots — cleared inlets, jetted connecting lines, verified outfalls — plus attention to the waterproofing around drain penetrations, which is where leaks into occupied space below usually start. If your garage has tide marks on the walls or efflorescence near drains, the system is already telling you it needs attention.
